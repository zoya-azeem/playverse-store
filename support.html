Refund policy

On a real storefront this is the policy we would run. It is reproduced here because a store page without one is a store page that is hiding something.

  • 48 hours, no questions. Any paid title can be refunded within 48 hours of purchase, whether or not you played it.
  • After 48 hours, refunds are considered case by case — a title that does not run on your device is always refunded, whenever you report it.
  • In-app purchases follow the same 48-hour window, except for consumables that have already been spent.
  • Repeated refunds of the same title are limited to two per account, to stop rent-and-return cycles.
  • Refunds return to the original payment method and typically take three to five working days to appear.

Demo note. No purchase is possible on this site, so nothing can be refunded. This section exists to show what the page would contain.

Content policy

What a title must do to stay listed:

Be honest about cost

The listing must state up front whether there are ads, in-app purchases or a subscription. A free title that gates its core loop behind a purchase after ten minutes is not free.

Protect children

Anything rated 3+ may not show interstitial ads, prompt for a purchase in the first ten minutes, or use countdown pressure. Chat with strangers requires a higher rating.

Ask only for what you need

Permissions must map to a visible feature. A torch app requesting contacts is removed, not warned.

No fake signals

Purchased reviews, install farms and misleading screenshots all mean removal. Appeals are read by a person, not an automated filter.

Publish a title

What a submission needs before review starts:

  • A build that runs on a mid-range device from three years ago, not just a flagship
  • Store listing: title, one-line summary, full description, six screenshots and an icon
  • An honest content rating with the questionnaire completed
  • A privacy note listing every piece of data collected and why
  • A support email a human actually reads

Review takes two to five working days. Rejections say exactly which rule was missed and what would fix it — a rejection you cannot act on is a bug in our process.
